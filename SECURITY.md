# Security Policy

## Why this file is not boilerplate here

This package drives **your real browser, signed into everything you are signed into**. It is not a
headless browser in a container. A bug that lets a page, a prompt or a dependency steer the agent is a
bug that reaches your email, your source control and your bank, using your cookies. Please treat a
finding here as higher severity than the same finding in an ordinary CLI tool, and so will we.

## Reporting a vulnerability

**Do not open a public issue.**

Use GitHub's private reporting: **[Report a vulnerability](https://github.com/farazpawle/automatebrowser/security/advisories/new)**.
It is private to the maintainers, gives us a place to work with you, and lets us credit you when the
fix ships. If that form is unavailable to you, open a public issue containing only "security report,
please contact me" and nothing else, and we will open a private channel.

Please include, as far as you have it:

- What an attacker gets, in one sentence.
- The version (`automate-browser --version`, or the `version` in `package.json`), your OS, and your
  browser.
- Steps to reproduce, or a proof of concept. A failing test is ideal.
- Whether it needs the user to have opted into anything — advanced mode, a non-loopback relay bind,
  a disabled sandbox — because that is what decides severity.

## What happens next

| Stage | Target |
|---|---|
| We acknowledge your report | 3 working days |
| We confirm or reject it, with reasoning | 10 working days |
| Fix released for a confirmed high-severity issue | 30 days of confirmation |
| Public advisory | With the fix, crediting you unless you prefer otherwise |

This is a small project, maintained by one person. If a deadline is going to slip you will be told
rather than left waiting. If you get no acknowledgement in 10 days, please escalate by opening the
placeholder issue described above.

## Scope

**In scope** — anything that crosses a boundary this project claims to hold:

- Escaping the **file-path sandbox** (`browser_upload_file`, or any tool taking a `filePath`).
- Reaching the relay's WebSocket port from **outside the machine**, or without the shared token when
  one is set.
- Bypassing the **origin allow/deny list**, read-only mode, or the switch that forbids agent-written
  JavaScript.
- Getting **redacted values** (cookies, storage, headers) into a reply that did not ask for them, or
  into the audit log.
- A **page** persuading the agent to act outside the tab it owns, or to take over a tab it does not.
- Privilege escalation through the extension, or through a message the relay forwards without
  validating.

**Out of scope** — the documented shape of the product, not bugs:

- Anything reachable only after the user sets `AUTOMATE_BROWSER_ALLOW_UNRESTRICTED_PATHS=1`. That
  switch exists to turn the sandbox off, and says so.
- Advanced mode's use of the Chrome debugger, which shows a banner and is opt-in per session.
- Binding the relay to a non-loopback address, which already refuses to start without a token.
- The agent doing something harmful because a user told it to. The trust boundary is the page and the
  network, not the person holding the keyboard.
- Reports generated by a scanner with no working proof of concept, and dependency advisories with no
  reachable path in this code. We run `npm audit --omit=dev` in CI on every push.

## Safe harbour

Research in good faith against **your own** browser and your own machine is welcome and we will not
pursue it. Do not test against anyone else's data, do not use a finding to reach an account that is
not yours, and give us a chance to fix it before publishing.
